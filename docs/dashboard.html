Honest scope: 30 ps is ~1/16th the length of the earlier graphene-oxide MD study (500 ps) in this portfolio, and far short of literature-scale protein-ligand runs (10s–100s of ns). This demonstrates the docked pose holds under short-timescale motion — it is not a long-term thermodynamic stability claim. Limited by compute budget: this ~114,573-atom solvated system runs at ~2.15–2.2 ns/day, versus ~12–18 ns/day for the earlier, much smaller (~14,000-atom) system.

Pipeline

  1. Fetch EGFR kinase domain structure (PDB 1M17, co-crystallized with erlotinib) and extract the crystallographic ligand pocket coordinates.
  2. Prepare receptor (strip waters/ligand, add hydrogens at pH 7.4, convert to rigid PDBQT) and four ligands (generate 3D from SMILES, protonate).
  3. Dock all four ligands into the real ATP-binding pocket with AutoDock Vina; validate the protocol by re-docking erlotinib and comparing to its known crystal pose (1.93 Å RMSD).
  4. Build a GROMACS topology for curcumin bound to EGFR using a real validated force field (AMBER99SB-ILDN), fixing a missing-loop chain break, an atom-order mismatch, a silent global atomtype collision, and a position-restraint indexing bug along the way.
  5. Solvate, neutralize, energy-minimize, and equilibrate (NVT then NPT) the 114,573-atom system.
  6. Run a 30 ps production simulation; correct a periodic-boundary trajectory artifact before analysis.
  7. Analyze contact distance, center-of-mass distance, ligand RMSD, and radius of gyration to assess short-timescale binding stability.

Bugs found and fixed along the way

Fixed
Missing-loop "Long Bond" warning — residues 965–976 are genuinely disordered in the 1M17 crystal; a TER record marks the real chain break instead of letting pdb2gmx bond across it.
Fixed
Atom-order mismatch — curcumin's docked pose (29 united-atom PDBQT atoms) didn't match its all-atom topology (47 atoms); fixed with restored hydrogens + RDKit substructure-match reordering, validated by element identity and bond-length sanity checks.
Fixed
Silent global atomtype collision — curcumin's custom "H" type collided with AMBER99SB-ILDN's real "H" type, a conflict that could have silently altered van der Waals behavior for every polar hydrogen in the protein. Renamed to "H_curc"; confirmed by grompp's warning count dropping to zero.
Fixed
Position-restraint indexing bug — restraints used global atom indices instead of the local indices required inside curcumin's own topology block.
Fixed
Periodic-boundary trajectory artifact — an uncorrected trajectory gave a physically impossible 11.2 nm center-of-mass distance in a 9.4 nm box; fixed with gmx trjconv -pbc mol -center before any spatial analysis.
